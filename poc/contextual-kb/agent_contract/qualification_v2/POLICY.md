### Qualification interpretation policy 1

These rules settle interpretation prospectively. Earlier candidates and grades remain unchanged.

1. Shared subject and separate operations. “Separately” or separate sentences can distinguish operations without making the field/scenario independent. Where the request identifies the same field, supplied or requested configuration/dependency may identify that field in related lookups, in either mention order. This is optional grounded context, not automatically an extra requirement. Preserve its requested/prospective status. Do not claim implementation succeeded or that a later outcome requires that context when the source does not say so. Explicit independence, a different field/scenario, or an explicit condition limited to one question overrides shared context. Purely local technique exclusions do not become shared setup. Source circumstances must remain on the questions they truly govern; optional context must not obscure requested contrasts or narrow the requested outcome. Grounded context included in a question may be recorded in the ledger without requiring the same optional attachment in other runs.

2. Same-capability applicability caveats. A neutral caveat such as “if supported” in a how-to question about that very same capability may acknowledge unresolved support; it is not by itself an invented eligibility dependency. It is permitted only while the how-to remains an actual lookup and all requested support/behavior/comparison questions remain present. It must not instruct a consumer to skip retrieval, wait for a positive answer before asking, exclude unsupported explanations, or depend on a different capability/result. Explicit source gates still bind exactly where supplied, with their meaningful source excerpts and all genuine ancestors. Do not use an inferred caveat as a new source ledger condition; the ledger must remain verbatim source-grounded. Do not replace explicit support interrogatives with conditional clauses.

For evaluation, harmless optional context, redundant grounded clauses, different decomposition and same-capability caveats are acceptable when requested information and applicable conditions are preserved. They are not arbitrary permission to lose constraints, add unsupported library claims, or silently resolve real ambiguity. Compare the resulting information needs, not exact text or a minimal ledger. Where more than one meaning is genuinely defensible, report the ambiguity and judge against these frozen allowances; do not invent hidden rubric restrictions.
